# About blocks for the 24 types without a buying guide (drafts, local, 2026-10-15)

Each is shown on page 1 of the type page, under the grid, followed by an automatic sentence from the catalogue (countries, independent makers, new pieces). The one-line preamble under the title is added too. Nothing is on the live site yet.

## Wall lamps (/work/wall-lamps.html)
A wall lamp lights from the side of a room instead of from the ceiling or a table. That frees the floor and puts the light where it is used: beside a bed, over a mirror, along a hallway or above a desk. They range from small fixed sconces and swing-arm reading lights to long linear wall lights, and from lamps wired into the wall to plug-in designs with a visible cable.

## Ceiling lamps (/work/ceiling-lamps.html)
A ceiling lamp is fixed straight to the ceiling with no drop, which makes it the choice for low rooms, hallways and kitchens where a hanging lamp would be in the way. They include flush mounts and surface mounts, from simple discs and domes to sculpted shades and multi-arm fittings.

## Chandeliers (/work/chandeliers.html)
A chandelier is a ceiling lamp with several arms, lights or elements, made to be looked at as much as to give light. Classic crystal and glass designs sit beside contemporary ones in metal, blown glass, paper and ceramic, in sizes from a compact cluster over a table to a centrepiece for a stairwell.

## Garden and outdoor chairs (/work/garden-chairs.html)
Garden and outdoor chairs are made to stay outside for part of the year, so the material matters: teak and other hardwoods, powder-coated aluminium and steel, woven synthetic fibre, stone and weather-resistant fabric. They range from folding bistro chairs to deep lounge chairs and dining chairs for a terrace table.

## Side chairs (/work/side-chairs.html)
A side chair is a lighter chair that is not tied to one table. It can stand against a wall in a hallway, sit at a desk or add a seat in a living room, and many are also used at dining tables. They range from slim wood and metal frames to upholstered seats, with and without arms.

## Folding and stacking chairs (/work/folding-chairs.html)
Folding and stacking chairs are designed to be put away: a folding chair flattens against a wall and stacking chairs sit in a pile. They suit small homes, extra guests, studios and cafés. They range from classic wooden folding chairs to moulded stacking chairs and light metal designs.

## Kids' chairs (/work/kids-chairs.html)
Kids' chairs are made smaller and lower than adult chairs, so a child can sit with their feet on the floor. They include scaled-down versions of well-known chairs, stackable chairs for a play room, stools and high chairs, in wood, plastic and upholstery.

## Office and desk chairs (/work/office-chairs.html)
Office and desk chairs are made to be sat in for hours: most swivel and adjust in height, and many also tilt or have adjustable arms and backs. The page also lists desk chairs for the home, from task chairs with mesh backs to upholstered designs that look at home in a living room.

## Dining chairs (/work/dining-chairs.html)
A dining chair is sat in for a meal and often for the hour after it, so seat height, depth and back support matter as much as looks. They range from slim wood and cane chairs to upholstered and moulded designs, with and without arms, and many come in several finishes.

## Benches (/work/benches.html)
A bench seats two or more people along one length. It works at a dining table in place of chairs, at the end of a bed, in a hallway for shoes and coats, or against a wall as extra seating. They range from solid wood and stone benches to upholstered and outdoor designs, some with storage.

## Footstools (/work/footstools.html)
A footstool is a low seat or rest for the feet, made as the companion to an armchair or lounge chair. Some are sold as a matching piece; others are stand-alone designs in leather, wool, wood or padded fabric that also work as an extra seat or a small table.

## Ottomans and poufs (/work/ottomans-and-poufs.html)
Ottomans and poufs are low, soft seats without a back. They are used as extra seating, as a footrest or, with a tray on top, as a small table. They range from round cushioned poufs in wool, leather and knit to large rectangular ottomans, some with a hinged lid and storage inside.

## Console tables (/work/console-tables.html)
A console table is a narrow table made to stand against a wall: in a hallway, behind a sofa or under a mirror. It holds keys, a lamp, a bowl or books without taking much floor space. They range from slim wood and metal designs to marble-topped and sculptural ones, with and without drawers.

## Bedside tables (/work/bedside-tables.html)
A bedside table holds what you reach for at night: a lamp, a book, a glass of water, a phone. They are made to sit close to the height of the mattress. They range from open tables and trays on legs to cabinets with drawers or doors, and wall-hung designs that leave the floor clear.

## Outdoor tables (/work/outdoor-tables.html)
Outdoor tables are made for terraces, balconies and gardens, in materials that stand up to rain, sun and frost: hardwood, aluminium, steel, stone, ceramic and weather-resistant resin. They range from small bistro tables to long dining tables, and from low tables for a lounge group to side tables.

## Bar tables (/work/bar-tables.html)
A bar table is taller than a dining table, made to be used standing or with bar stools or counter-height chairs. It suits kitchen islands, small flats and places for entertaining. They range from small round pub-style tables to long counters, in wood, metal and stone.

## Candles (/work/candles.html)
The candles here range from scented candles in glass, ceramic and tin to plain taper and pillar candles, and candles sold as objects in their own right. Scent, burn time and wax differ from maker to maker, so each piece links to the maker's own page for the details.

## Glassware (/work/glass.html)
This page gathers glassware: drinking glasses, wine and water glasses, tumblers, carafes and jugs, made by hand or machine. They range from thin, mouth-blown glass to heavy pressed and coloured glass, and from single glasses to sets.

## Vases (/work/vases.html)
A vase holds flowers, branches or dried stems, and is often an object worth looking at without them. They range from small bud vases to tall floor vases, in ceramic, glass, metal and stone, and from smooth thrown shapes to hand-built and sculptural forms.

## Bowls (/work/bowls.html)
Bowls work on the table and on the shelf: serving and salad bowls, soup and cereal bowls, fruit bowls, and bowls that are simply objects. They range from thrown ceramic and glass to wood, metal and stone, in sizes from a small dish to a wide centrepiece.

## Plates (/work/plates.html)
Plates range from dinner and side plates to platters, serving plates and decorative wall plates. They are made in porcelain, stoneware, earthenware and glass, plain or patterned, and are often sold singly as well as in sets.

## Trays (/work/trays.html)
A tray carries, serves and gathers: breakfast and drinks, small things on a table, or keys and post by the door. They range from small catch-all trays to large serving trays, in wood, metal, ceramic, leather and woven materials.

## Cushions (/work/cushions.html)
Cushions add comfort and colour to a sofa, bed or chair. They range from wool, linen, cotton and velvet to knitted, quilted and printed designs, and from square and rectangular to round and bolster shapes. Some are sold as a cover only, without the inner.

## Blankets and throws (/work/blankets.html)
Blankets and throws are for the sofa, the end of a bed or a cold evening. They range from wool, mohair and cashmere to cotton, linen and recycled fibres, in woven, knitted and quilted forms, and from plain to patterned designs.
