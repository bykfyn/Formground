Search finds what's popular. Formground finds what's real.

Type what you're looking for — a material, a form, a feeling — and see furniture, lighting, ceramics, and objects from the people who actually make them. Not what's best-optimized to show up. What's real.

Try a search

The problem

Search for a specific kind of chair, and you'll get retailers before you get makers. Not because the makers aren't out there — because search rewards whoever's easiest to find, and small studios rarely have the marketing budget to compete for that.

Formground looks somewhere else: directly at the maker's own site — their real work, in their own words, whether or not they've ever optimized for anything.

How it works

Ask, plainly.

No filters, no categories to navigate. Describe what you're after, the way you'd describe it to a person.

Get real results, not rankings.

Every result comes from a maker's own site. Nobody paid to be here, and nobody paid to be shown first.

Follow it straight to the source.

Every result links back to where it came from. Formground doesn't sell anything — it just helps you find who does.

Why smaller makers show up here

Most discovery tools require a maker to apply, submit a catalog, or maintain a profile. Formground doesn't ask that. If it's on a maker's own site, it can be found here — a single hand-carved piece from someone with no website beyond a simple portfolio page shows up with the same weight as a brand with a full catalog of furniture and lighting.

That's deliberate. A maker shouldn't need a marketing team to be findable.

For makers

You don't need to do anything to be found here — nothing to submit, nothing to maintain. If your work is real and it's online, it's discoverable.

If people are finding you through Formground, we'll tell you. No dashboard, no login — just a straightforward summary of who found you and how, sent directly.

Don't want to be included? Say the word and you're removed, no questions asked.

Built for people and for the tools people use

Formground works the same way whether a person types a search or an AI assistant asks on someone's behalf. Same real results either way — nothing changes based on who's asking.

Where things stand

Formground is early — a small, growing index of independent furniture, lighting, ceramics, and object makers, built one search at a time. It'll never be exhaustive. It's trying to be honest instead.
