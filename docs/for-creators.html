Designing & prototyping

CAD software for building and testing a design digitally before committing to materials or tooling.

Shapeways

Upload a digital model, get it 3D printed and shipped — useful for a physical prototype before a full run.

Materials

A materials database covering new and sustainable options, built for designers rather than manufacturers.

Graphic design & branding

99designs

Run a design contest for a logo or brand identity rather than a single quote from one designer.

Fiverr

A freelancer marketplace if you'd rather hire one designer directly for a logotype or brand work.

Hosting

WP Engine

Managed WordPress hosting built specifically for it — pairs well with WooCommerce, and costs more than generic hosting but removes most of the server maintenance a self-hosted store otherwise needs.

Cloudways

A more affordable managed alternative — still handles server upkeep, with a choice of underlying cloud providers.

Selling online

Shopify

The most common platform among makers already on Formground — a full online store, no coding required.

Squarespace

Popular with makers who want a portfolio-style site alongside a shop — several Formground makers use it already.

WooCommerce

A WordPress plugin rather than a hosted platform — also well represented among makers already on Formground.

Big Cartel

Built specifically for independent artists and makers with a small catalog, rather than general retail.

Etsy

A marketplace rather than your own store, and a better fit for smaller-ticket handmade craft than the higher-end design found here. Fees and ads can also take close to a third of revenue, so most makers here would treat it as a supplement to their own site at most, if at all.

Wholesale & retail distribution

These are wholesale/B2B channels, not a replacement for selling direct — a natural next step once you've proven demand through your own site or Formground, connecting you with retailers rather than end customers.

Faire

A wholesale marketplace connecting independent brands with retailers, without needing your own sales team — strongest in North America.

Ankorstore

Similar to Faire, but built around European retailers and brands.

High-end & collectible marketplaces

Both below are vetted, application-based marketplaces — generally a better fit once you have an established track record than when you're just starting out. Formground and these serve largely different tiers of maker, not the same audience.

1stDibs

A marketplace for high-end and collectible design, reaching buyers who aren't necessarily searching by brand name.

Pamono

A similar collectible-design marketplace, mainly reaching European buyers.

Building demand before a drop

Klaviyo

Email and SMS tools for building a waitlist ahead of a small, limited run — an alternative to relying on social media alone.

Product photography

PhotoRoom

AI-based background removal and product photo editing — a lower-cost alternative to a full studio shoot.

Packaging

Packhelp

Custom packaging in small quantities — useful when pieces vary in size and off-the-shelf boxes don't fit well.

Shipping & fulfillment

Shippo

Compares shipping rates across carriers for smaller parcels — strongest carrier coverage in the US and Canada.

Sendcloud

A similar shipping tool built around European carriers — a better fit than Shippo for makers based in the EU.

uShip

A freight marketplace built for large, heavy, or fragile items — a better fit for furniture than standard parcel carriers, mainly used in North America and Europe.

Product regulation

Product-transparency regulation is on the way in several markets, and the EU is furthest along. Its Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) is gradually introducing a "Digital Product Passport" (DPP) — a standard digital record of a product's materials, durability, and repairability, meant to travel with it. Furniture is one of the categories in scope; the EU's central DPP registry went live in July 2026, and furniture-specific requirements are expected to follow around 2028–2029. That's a few years out, but worth knowing about now — wherever you sell from, since other markets are likely to introduce their own versions over time.

The regulation is meant to scale with company size, and EU member states are required to offer smaller businesses guidance and support — but tracking a regulation like this alone is still a lot to ask of a one-person studio. This is one area where a trade association can genuinely help: several furniture and textile industry bodies are already building shared Digital Product Passport infrastructure so individual members don't each have to solve it from scratch. The European Furniture Industries Confederation (EFIC), for example, has been directly engaged in shaping how these rules apply to furniture makers. Being part of — or at least following — your national or sector association is a reasonable way to stay ahead of this.

This is general awareness, not legal advice — exact requirements are still being finalized and vary by product category. Check official sources or your own industry association for guidance specific to your business.

SEO basics for your own site

A few things make most of the difference: a clear, descriptive title and short description on every page, real alt text on every image, fast-loading photos, and one canonical page per item rather than duplicates. None of it needs an agency — most of it is a platform setting or a few minutes per page, and a general AI assistant can help draft or check things like alt text and page descriptions if writing isn't where you want to spend your time — just read over what it gives you before it goes live.

Google Search Console

Free — shows how Google actually sees your site, and lets you submit a sitemap or flag a page for re-indexing.

Google's SEO Starter Guide

Free, written by Google itself, and enough to cover the fundamentals without hiring anyone.

Being found by AI search & agentic tools

A growing share of searches now happen through an AI assistant asking on someone's behalf rather than a person typing into Google — the same shift Formground was built around. What helps a human find your work also helps an AI agent do the same: clean, factual pages with real names, materials, and images, plus structured data an assistant can read without guessing. Nothing exotic required — just a well-built page rather than a wall of marketing copy.

schema.org structured data

The structured-data format search engines and AI agents both use to read a page accurately - Product for makers, but schema.org covers people, services, and organizations just as well.
